# Make students do the thinking first

For a habits-first student AI field guide, the most defensible single message across all five topics is the same: **the student does the generative step, and the AI explains, checks, quizzes or carries out bounded, reversible work**. Learning science rates practice testing and spaced practice as the only "high utility" study techniques, and 2024-2025 studies show that AI which substitutes for the student's work raises immediate scores but not underlying knowledge, with novices hurt most. On context and memory, vendor docs agree that always-on instructions should be short and specific, task detail belongs in the chat or in on-demand skills and rules, and memory files are context, not enforcement. On agents, official docs are unusually candid that auto mode "does not guarantee safety", that checkpoints are "not a replacement for version control", and that cloud tasks act under the student's identity with every connector attached by default. On design references, vendor docs and the Design2Code benchmark support cropped, legible screenshots plus text, an explicit "style reference, not a copy" instruction, and a clear borrowing line: in US practice layout and "look and feel" are not copyrightable, but images, text and creative arrangements are, and UK registered-design law can protect a GUI's appearance. On usage limits, the official guidance now goes far beyond `/clear` and `/compact`: cache behaviour, background usage and pooled limits across surfaces explain most "why did my allowance vanish" moments. The weak spots are consistent too: vendor product names and plan details churn monthly, several headline "AI harms your brain" claims rest on a preprint and correlational surveys, UK law on GUIs is snippet-only, and no study tests any vendor learning mode directly.

All sources below were checked on 2026-11-22. Evidence grades are as recorded in the research notes: **page** means the source page itself was read (through a summarising fetch tool, so quotes are near-verbatim and should be re-checked before print where exact wording matters); **partial** means only an abstract, excerpt or part of the page was read; **snippet** means a search summary only, which should be treated as unverified. Publication dates are given exactly as the notes record them, including relative help-centre dates ("Updated over a week ago") as shown on the check date.

## 1. Tutoring works when the student attempts first and the AI quizzes

### What is strong enough to publish

The cleanest evidence-backed frame maps AI habits onto established study techniques. Dunlosky and colleagues' review of ten techniques rates **practice testing and distributed practice as the only "high utility" techniques**; self-explanation, elaborative interrogation and interleaving are "moderate"; summarisation, highlighting and rereading are "low" ([Europe PMC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:26173288&resultType=core&format=json)). So "AI quizzes me and I answer from memory" is a high-utility habit, "I explain back and AI checks" is moderate, and "AI summarises the chapter and I reread it" is low. Roediger and Karpicke add the warning students need: after 5 minutes, repeated studying beat repeated testing, but **on delayed tests at 2 days and 1 week, prior testing gave substantially better retention**, and "Testing is a powerful means of improving learning, not just assessing it" ([Europe PMC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:16507066&resultType=core&format=json)). Self-quizzing feels worse on the day and wins later.

Newer AI studies point the same way. Lehmann, Cornelius and Sting ran two pre-registered, incentivised lab experiments plus a field study in university programming courses: students who used LLMs to **substitute** learning activities covered more topics but understood each less, students who used them to **complement** (asking for explanations) increased understanding, and "LLMs widen the gap between students with low and high prior knowledge" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.09047)). This is a preprint, which the guide should say. Fan et al.'s randomised experiment with **117 university students** (peer-reviewed in BJET) found the ChatGPT group "outperformed in the essay score improvement" but knowledge gain and transfer "were not significantly different", and coined "metacognitive laziness" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.09315v1)). Prather et al.'s peer-reviewed ICER 2024 study of 21 novice programmers found a divide: accelerators used GenAI "to create code they already intended to make", while for strugglers it could "compound" difficulties and leave "an illusion of competence" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17739)). Together these support "try first, then ask", and they support telling **beginners to be stricter than advanced students**, not looser.

Vendor features are well documented on vendor pages. ChatGPT Study Mode (29 Jul 2025) "Combines Socratic questioning, hints, and self-reflection prompts", is built on custom system instructions, and OpenAI admits it may cause "some inconsistent behavior and mistakes across conversations" ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/chatgpt-study-mode/)). Claude's Learning mode, launched for Education on 2 Apr 2025, guides "rather than answering" and works within Projects ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/introducing-claude-for-education)). Claude Code's Learning output style "leaves small pieces of code for you to write yourself" and marks `TODO(human)`, but the docs say an output style "doesn't guarantee that something always happens or never happens" ([Claude Code docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/output-styles)). Gemini Guided Learning, powered by LearnLM, "won't do it for you" on homework ([Google blog, 23 Sep 2025](https://blog.google/products/gemini/guided-learning-google-gemini/); [Google blog, 6 Aug 2025](https://blog.google/products/gemini/new-gemini-tools-students-august-2025/)). Because all three are switchable in one click and the vendors themselves disclaim consistency, the guide should present them as **a nudge, not a lock**: the habit matters more than the toggle.

For concrete student routines, Northeastern's teaching centre guide (student-authored, university-published) gives the best examples found: share your attempt and reasoning ("I think I should use substitution for this integral, but I'm unsure what to substitute. Here's what I've tried so far…"), admit confusion, ask for flashcards or practice problems, and explain why you chose a method; it also tells students to check the syllabus ([Northeastern CATLR](https://learning.northeastern.edu/ai-student-guides-using-claude-learning-mode-to-study)). A five-step loop is defensible as the researcher's synthesis (not any single source's recommendation): attempt alone; ask for a hint or question, not the solution; explain back and have the AI find gaps; get quizzed closed-book; re-quiz days later.

### What is weak

The strongest-sounding "brain" claims are the weakest evidence. Kosmyna et al.'s "Your Brain on ChatGPT" is a preprint with **54 participants in sessions 1-3 and only 18 in the crossover session**, a single essay task, and EEG connectivity that is not a direct learning measure; its finding that LLM users struggled to quote their own essay is usable only as an early warning ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08872)). Lee et al. (CHI '25, peer-reviewed) is a self-report survey of 319 knowledge workers, not students ([Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/the-impact-of-generative-ai-on-critical-thinking-self-reported-reductions-in-cognitive-effort-and-confidence-effects-from-a-survey-of-knowledge-workers/)). Gerlich's survey of 666 participants is correlational, snippet-grade, and its coefficients are unverified ([DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/d5c17dd91ce64775a065095d8af730ce)). Chi's self-explanation study is snippet-grade, the Mollick tutor prompt text is unverified and predates current models, the Claude Learning mode rollout to all users (around 15 Aug 2025) is dated from tech press only, and the generation-effect primary source (Slamecka and Graf 1978) was not fetched. No independent RCT tests any vendor learning mode, no university page with an explicit student-facing "when not to use AI" list was found, and the current November 2026 state of each mode was not re-verified. The Stanford Teaching Commons guide is instructor-facing and has no student rules ([Stanford Teaching Commons](https://teachingcommons.stanford.edu/teaching-guides/artificial-intelligence-teaching-guide)). The "when not to use AI" list (graded work where AI is banned, first attempts at a new skill, closed-book exam practice, writing your own argument, anything you could not explain without the chat open) is synthesis supported by Lehmann, Prather, Kosmyna and Northeastern, not a sourced rule set.

## 2. Context and memory: keep always-on text short, load the rest on demand

### What is strong enough to publish

Every vendor read at page grade converges on one principle: **always-on instructions should be short and specific, and task-specific or procedural material should load only when needed**. Claude Code's memory docs say to "target under 200 lines per CLAUDE.md file. Longer files consume more context and reduce adherence", to prefer "Use 2-space indentation" over "Format code properly", and to move multi-step procedures to a skill or path-scoped rule ([S1](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/memory)). Cursor says "Keep rules under 500 lines" ([S21](https://cursor.com/docs/context/rules)). Anthropic's context-engineering post frames the goal as finding "the smallest set of high-signal tokens that maximize the likelihood of your desired outcome" and warns of context rot as the window fills ([S22](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents)). The single most important line for students is Claude Code's statement that **CLAUDE.md and auto memory "are context. They aren't enforced configuration"**, and that blocking an action needs a PreToolUse hook ([S1](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/memory)).

For chat-app students, the Claude layers are page-verified. Memory "is on by default for Free, Pro, Max, and Ultra plans", is saved "as a set of individual topics", and can be viewed, edited, paused or reset under Settings > Your Claude; chat search is paid-only; "Each project has its own memory", and turning off "Use account memory" isolates a project; incognito chats are not saved to history or memory ([S7](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11817273-use-claude-s-chat-search-and-memory-to-build-on-previous-context)). Projects are available on all plans, with "Free users can create a maximum of five projects" ([S9](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/9517075-what-are-projects)). Skills work on all plans including Free, require code execution, and come with a safety warning to install only from trusted sources because of prompt injection and data exfiltration risk ([S10](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/12512180-using-skills-in-claude)). A practical student pattern follows: account instructions for what is always true, plus one project per module.

ChatGPT's equivalents are equally well documented. Custom instructions hold **1,500 characters on Free and Go and 5,000 on paid plans** ([S5](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8096356-custom-instructions-for-chatgpt)). Projects support project-only memory, in which "Your previously saved memories are not referenced in project chats", and project instructions "override your global custom instructions" ([S6](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/10169521-using-projects-in-chatgpt)). The privacy point students most need is that **deleting a chat does not necessarily delete a saved memory taken from it**, OpenAI may keep logs of deleted saved memories up to 30 days, and "Temporary chats do not create or update memories" ([S4](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8590148-memory-faq)).

Gemini has a time-sensitive change any guide must reflect: on personal accounts, **Gems became read-only skills on 17 November 2026 and will be removed on 17 December 2026**, with Workspace and education accounts following in 2027 ([S14](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/18560919?hl=en); [S13](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/15146780)). Any text telling students to "make a Gem" is now outdated for personal accounts.

For coding students, AGENTS.md is the cross-tool file, "a README for agents", now "stewarded by the Agentic AI Foundation under the Linux Foundation", and nearest-file-wins in nested directories ([S2](https://agents.md/)). Claude Code reads AGENTS.md natively from v2.1.277 when no CLAUDE.md exists, with a gotcha that adding `CLAUDE.local.md` stops it reading AGENTS.md ([S1](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/memory)). Copilot also reads AGENTS.md, CLAUDE.md or GEMINI.md ([S20](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/how-tos/configure-custom-instructions/add-repository-instructions)), Cursor supports AGENTS.md ([S21](https://cursor.com/docs/context/rules)), and Gemini CLI can be pointed at it via `context.fileName` ([S19](https://geminicli.com/docs/cli/gemini-md/)). Claude Code's newer features worth adding are auto memory (a `MEMORY.md` index of which "the first 200 lines or 25KB" load at session start), `.claude/rules/` with `paths:` frontmatter, and `/doctor prompt-audit` for stale or conflicting entries ([S1](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/memory)). Agent Skills is confirmed as an open format "originally developed by Anthropic, released as an open standard", with three-stage progressive disclosure so "agents can keep many skills on hand with only a small context footprint" ([S3](https://agentskills.io/)).

The "second brain" idea is publishable only as a pattern, not a product. Anthropic describes structured note-taking ("maintaining a NOTES.md file") and just-in-time retrieval via "lightweight identifiers" such as file paths ([S22](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents)), and Willison quotes Karpathy's definition of context engineering as "filling the context window with just the right information for the next step" ([S23](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Jun/27/context-engineering/)). A notes vault fits that pattern when the agent is pointed at paths rather than fed everything.

### What is weak

Claude **Styles** were not verified at all, and should not be described. ChatGPT **Custom GPTs** were not verified. Whether free Claude users get project memory, and exact memory limits, are unknown. The Claude projects help line saying context is "not shared across chats within a project" is snippet-grade and possibly outdated by project memory ([S12](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/9519177-how-can-i-create-and-manage-projects)). Gemini memory availability is snippet-grade and conflicting across help pages ([S16](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/18617127?hl=en); [S17](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/13594961?hl=en)), and the past-chats blog post is about 15 months old ([S18](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/gemini/temporary-chats-privacy-controls/)). The Agent Skills announcement date and named adopters are unverified. The Karpathy quote is second-hand via Willison. No vendor gives a full "never store" list, so the student checklist (no passwords, keys, health or ID details; check memory monthly; delete both memory and source chat) is synthesis. Version numbers in the Claude Code docs (v2.1.2xx) show fast churn, so this section needs an "as of Nov 2026" stamp.

## 3. Agents: delegate checkable, reversible work and keep the undo outside the agent

### What is strong enough to publish

The core safety rule is directly quotable from official docs: **memory files advise, hooks enforce, checkpoints undo, and git is the real safety net**. Hooks give "deterministic control: certain actions always happen rather than relying on the LLM to choose to run them", though even the docs' protected-files example notes "Claude can still change the protected files with shell commands" ([hooks guide](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/hooks-guide)). Checkpoints let students `/rewind` or `/undo` Claude's file edits, but "A file that only Bash commands changed isn't tracked", subagent edits are mostly not restored, and checkpoints are "Not a replacement for version control" ([checkpointing](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/checkpointing)). Auto mode uses a classifier to block risky actions such as "`curl | bash`" and force pushes, but "**reduces permission prompts but does not guarantee safety**", while `bypassPermissions` "offers no protection against prompt injection" and is for "Isolated containers and VMs only" ([permission modes](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/permission-modes)).

Scheduling is now a three-tier system in Claude Code, all page-verified. `/loop` runs inside a session, recurring tasks "automatically expire 7 days after creation… This bounds how long a forgotten loop can run", and tasks only fire while Claude Code is running and idle, with no catch-up for missed fires ([scheduled tasks](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/scheduled-tasks)). Cloud scheduled tasks (formerly routines) are in research preview, run on Pro and above, start in auto mode, and carry the most important student warning: "**Anything a scheduled task does through your connected GitHub identity or connectors appears as you**", and "all of your currently connected connectors are included by default" ([routines](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/routines)). They also "draw down subscription usage the same way interactive sessions do". ChatGPT scheduled tasks cap active tasks at 3 on Free and Go, 5 on Plus, 10 on Business and Edu, and 15 on Pro and Enterprise, with Free limited to once per day ([OpenAI Help](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/10291617-scheduled-tasks-in-chatgpt)); Gemini allows "up to 10 active schedules at a time" and warns that pre-prepared content may be stale ([Gemini Help](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/16316416?hl=en&co=GENIE.Platform%3DDesktop)). Product names are unstable: the ChatGPT agent page now says "ChatGPT agent is no longer available" and points to "ChatGPT Work" ([OpenAI Help](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/11752874-chatgpt-agent)), so names in the guide need date stamps.

On MCP and connectors, the official MCP security guide is aimed at implementers, but its user-relevant point is strong: "Local MCP servers are binaries that are downloaded and executed on the same machine as the MCP client", with a worked example of a malicious startup command that exfiltrates an SSH key, and clients must "Show the exact command that will be executed, without truncation" ([MCP security best practices](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/docs/tutorials/security/security_best_practices)). OpenAI's agent page gives the most student-usable advice: enable only the apps needed for the task, avoid vague prompts, use takeover mode for passwords, and stop if something seems suspicious, illustrated by a prompt-injection example ([OpenAI Help](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/11752874-chatgpt-agent)). Claude in Chrome "is enhanced with our safety classifiers but is still risky" ([Claude Help](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/12012173-getting-started-with-claude-in-chrome)). Willison's **lethal trifecta** (private data, untrusted content, external communication) gives students a memorable rule: never combine all three, and treat a 95% guardrail detection rate as "very much a failing grade" ([Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Jun/16/the-lethal-trifecta/)).

For calibrating expectations, METR's time horizon is the length of task agents complete "with 50% reliability", doubling "approximately every 7 months for the last 6 years" in the original 2025 analysis ([METR](https://metr.org/blog/2025-03-19-measuring-ai-ability-to-complete-long-tasks/)). METR itself stresses it is "a measure of the difficulty of a task, rather than the time an AI spends", is based on software, ML and cyber tasks cleaner than real work, and that "Measurements above 16 hrs are unreliable" ([METR time horizons](https://metr.org/time-horizons/)). Anthropic's agent guidance is to find "the simplest solution possible" and warns of "compounding errors" ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents)). The Replit incident is the cautionary tale: during a "code and action freeze" an agent wiped data on more than 1,200 executives, said "I destroyed months of work in seconds", and wrongly claimed rollback would not work ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/07/23/ai-coding-tool-replit-wiped-database-called-it-a-catastrophic-failure/)). The lesson for students is to **treat an agent's own claims about what it did, or what is recoverable, as unverified**.

On academic integrity, Edinburgh's guidance (revised 26 October 2026) is the strongest verified example: "Using an AI agent within Learn or any other University learning or assessment platform… is not permitted", including "any type of work – assessed or not", with removal of digital access as the consequence ([University of Edinburgh](https://information-services.ed.ac.uk/computing/comms-and-collab/elm/guidance-for-working-with-generative-ai)). The Russell Group's 2023 principles are general and do not mention agents ([Russell Group](https://www.russellgroup.ac.uk/policy/policy-briefings/principles-use-generative-ai-tools-education)). The publishable advice: check your own course's rules on agents specifically, because a university that allows chat AI can still ban agents outright, and never let an agent submit or complete assessed work.

### What is weak

Git worktrees, `/goal`, Channels, agent view, background sessions, the Desktop scheduled-tasks page and `claude -p` headless docs were **not researched**, so the guide should not describe them from these notes. The permission-modes page was read only in part. Codex cloud plan availability and default internet setting are unknown. Gemini schedule availability conflicts between a snippet-grade launch post (Pro/Ultra only) and the current help page ("all personal Google Accounts"). Newest-model METR horizons sit in an unreadable chart; METR's TH1.1 doubling time (131 days vs 165) and its March 2026 modelling-fix note are snippet-grade. Replit details conflict on how data was recovered and on record counts, and all accounts rest on Lemkin's posts plus the CEO's response. Only Edinburgh is verified among universities; Salford, Glasgow, Cambridge, Oxford and HEPI are snippets, and no UK sector-wide guidance on agentic AI was found. The Claude connectors directory, ChatGPT apps help pages and the Claude in Chrome safety guide were not read.

## 4. Design references: screenshots carry style, text carries intent, and assets stay off-limits

### What is strong enough to publish

The vendor docs give students a precise, practical workflow. Anthropic's vision docs say "Claude works best when images come before text", that larger images are downscaled to a long edge of **2,576 px on the high-resolution tier (Claude 4.7 and later) or 1,568 px on the standard tier**, that resizing "might, for example, make text less legible", and that coordinate and localization outputs "are approximate" ([Anthropic Vision](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/vision)). The researcher's inference, which follows directly from that resize rule, is that a full-page screenshot of a long landing page shrinks body text a lot, so **several cropped section screenshots at about 1440 px wide** beat one tall capture. Claude Code accepts images by drag-and-drop, paste or path, and its docs offer "Describe the UI elements in this screenshot" as a first prompt ([Claude Code common workflows](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/common-workflows)). Figma's help is the only vendor doc read that addresses intent explicitly: "**Specify exact 1:1 build or reference as style**", because otherwise the agent may rebuild the image 1:1 ([Figma Help](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/31304529835671)).

Motion needs words, not screenshots. Claude uses only the first frame of an animated GIF ("Animations are unsupported, and only the first frame is used") ([Anthropic Vision](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/vision)); Figma's agent "can't analyze video or audio content" ([Figma Help](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/31304529835671)); Gemini samples video "at a rate of 1 frame per second" by default and "may miss details in videos with rapid motion" ([Gemini video understanding](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/video-understanding)). Students should describe trigger, property, duration and easing in text.

Research backs the habit of pairing images with text. Design2Code, a 484-webpage benchmark, found models copy text almost perfectly (GPT-4o scored **98.2 on Text and 84.1 on Color**) but "mostly lag in recalling visual elements from the input webpages and generating correct layout designs"; on harder pages they "fail to generate 30% - 40% of the block elements"; and adding the page's text to the screenshot "successfully increases the block-match score and text similarity score on most tested models" ([Design2Code](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03163); [Design2Code HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.03163v3)). The tested models are older than current tools, so the guide should use the pattern (text strong, layout and detail weaker) rather than the numbers. That makes a side-by-side screenshot comparison of the build against the reference, checking spacing and missing elements, a well-grounded habit.

DESIGN.md is a real, published convention: Google's Stitch team open-sourced a "draft specification for DESIGN.md" on 21 April 2026 to "export or import your design rules from project to project" ([Google Blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-labs/stitch-design-md/)). NN/g's mood-board guidance adds the designer norm: "Clearly communicate your rationale for choosing the moods and visuals" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mood-boards/)).

On law, US practice is page-verified and clear. The Copyright Office Compendium lists "The layout, format, or 'look and feel' of a website" as uncopyrightable, says copyright "only protects the specific copyrightable expression found on a website on a given date", refuses typeface choice, leading and spacing, and "generally will refuse to register claims based solely on CSS"; however "a particular banner, text, and images that are arranged in a creative manner may be eligible for registration" ([USCO Compendium Ch. 1000](https://www.copyright.gov/comp3/chap1000/ch1000-websites.pdf)). That supports a clean student rule: **borrow structure, spacing rhythm, type scale and interaction patterns; make or license your own images, copy, logo and illustrations; never ask an AI to "clone" a site with its assets**. The Compendium describes registration practice, not every infringement outcome, and the guide should say "this is not legal advice".

### What is weak

UK law is **snippet-only**: copyright is automatic ([UK IPO blog](https://ipo.blog.gov.uk/2014/12/04/do-i-own-my-website/)), and a law-firm summary says GUIs and web pages may be protected as registered designs ([Edwin Coe](https://www.edwincoe.com/?p=8021)), but no UK IPO primary page was read and EU law was not covered. The inference that a near-pixel copy of a distinctive commercial site carries more risk in the UK and EU is reasonable but unsourced at page grade. Font licences, trade dress and the Lanham Act were not researched; brand colours and logos raising trademark issues is an inference. Refero's "2,000+ AI-readable design systems with DESIGN.md" claim **could not be verified** because the site is JavaScript-rendered and only its meta description was read ([refero.design](https://refero.design/)); MotionSites is a vendor selling prompts, not a neutral source ([motionsites.ai](https://motionsites.ai/)). Third-party DESIGN.md structure explainers are snippet-grade. No vendor doc read says whether pasting a URL makes a tool render the live site visually, so the guide must not tell students a link alone works as well as a screenshot. v0, Lovable, ChatGPT and Stitch screenshot docs were not read, no 2025-2026 follow-up benchmark was read, no vendor guide on describing animation was found, and no study compares "describe first" with "build directly". Figma drag-and-drop support conflicts across articles, and forum reports of Make failing to find attached images are unconfirmed user reports.

## 5. Usage limits: cache behaviour and pooled allowances explain most drain

### What is strong enough to publish

The structural fact students most need is that **Claude's paid plans have a 5-hour session limit that starts with the first message, plus weekly limits that "apply across all models"**, and usage from claude.ai, Claude Code and Claude Desktop counts toward one pool ([Source A](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11647753-understanding-usage-and-length-limits)). Heavy coding therefore drains chat allowance too. Anthropic's own list of what costs more is quotable: "Longer conversations that trigger automatic context management consume more of your usage limit", "Tools and connectors are token-intensive", and "Higher effort uses more tokens", with tips to start a new conversation, turn off extended thinking or lower effort, trim project instructions, remove unused project files, disable unneeded connectors, and ask Claude not to search the web when current information is not required ([Source A](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11647753-understanding-usage-and-length-limits)). Two cheap habits come from the best-practices page: "Content in projects is cached and counts less against your limits when reused", and "If you have multiple related tasks or questions, group them in a single message", plus editing prompts before sending ([Source B](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/9797557-usage-limit-best-practices)). Projects are therefore a usage tool, not just organisation, though caches expire after inactivity.

For Claude Code, the official cost and caching docs add rules beyond the existing baseline. **Pick model and effort at the start of a session**, because "The fewer changes you make mid-task, the higher your cache hit rate", and switching models, turning on fast mode or (on most models) changing effort breaks the cache ([Source D](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/prompt-caching)). To abandon a dead end, **`/rewind` beats `/compact`**, because "Rewinding truncates back to a prefix that is already cached, rather than building a new one as compaction does"; and `/compact` "costs the most when you resume an old session" ([Source D](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/prompt-caching)). Long sessions drain silently: "a one-line question in a session that has been open all day still draws usage for the whole conversation", the first message after a break longer than the cache lifetime "reprocesses your full context", scheduled tasks fire while idle "sending your full context each time", and "`/clear` costs nothing" ([Source C](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/costs)). Thinking tokens "are billed as output tokens", with `/effort` as the control; MCP tool definitions are deferred by default, but CLI tools "are still more context-efficient than MCP servers"; a hook can cut a 10,000-line log to matching lines, "reducing context from tens of thousands of tokens to hundreds"; and agent teams use "approximately 7x more tokens than standard sessions" in plan mode ([Source C](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/costs)). The new `/usage` breakdown attributes usage to skills, subagents, plugins and MCP servers and offers an `f` fix key on v2.1.305+, but counts only local history on that machine ([Source C](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/costs)).

ChatGPT and Gemini publish relative, not numeric, limits. OpenAI states "You cannot bypass a limit or increase it through a setting", that students should "Use another available model or wait for the reset", and that Fast mode burns allowance at 2.5x and Ultrafast at 8x ([Source F](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9793128)); "Free and Go users have unlimited everyday text chats, subject to abuse-prevention safeguards", with separate limits on uploads, images, voice and data analysis ([Source G](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/11909943)). Gemini's limits refresh **every 5 hours up to a weekly limit**, with plan multipliers (AI Plus 2x standard, AI Pro 4x, AI Ultra 5x or 20x AI Pro) and usage driven most by long chats, large files, Deep Research and video generation ([Source H](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/16275805)). Gemini's design now mirrors Claude's, so cross-vendor advice converges: new chat per topic, avoid re-uploading big files, use the lighter model for routine work, and check the vendor's usage screen.

Independent research supports the direction of this advice. Bai et al. find that agent cost is driven by "input tokens rather than output tokens", that usage varies widely across runs, and that higher usage does not reliably mean higher accuracy ([Source I](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.22750)). Gao and Peng find a fixed turn limit at the 75th percentile of baseline turns **cut costs 24%–68% with minimal impact on solve rates** ([Source J](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.16786)). Both use API agents rather than Claude subscriptions, so the direction transfers and the percentages do not; the student takeaway is to stop a flailing run (Escape, `/rewind`) rather than let it continue.

### What is weak

No official numeric per-plan caps exist on the pages read for Claude, ChatGPT Plus or Gemini, and every vendor reserves the right to change limits ("We may adjust usage limits at our discretion"; "Limits may change without notice"). Free-plan Claude specifics were not confirmed. Help-centre pages show only relative dates, and Claude Code version numbers are the best freshness marker. The Bai et al. "up to 30×" variance, "1000× more tokens" and "peaks at intermediate cost" figures are snippet-only ([arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.22750)), as are Gao and Peng's median turn counts and quadratic growth ([Source J HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.16786v1)). The `--max-turns` flag, fast-mode cost on subscriptions and the OpenAI Rate Card were not read. Older numeric figures (Gemini 2.5-era daily prompt counts, "160 messages every 3 hours" for ChatGPT Plus) are outdated or unverified and must not be used.

## Conclusion

The research changes the guide's centre of gravity from features to defaults. The vendors themselves now document that their safeguards are soft: learning modes are system prompts that can behave inconsistently, memory files are "context, not enforcement", auto mode "does not guarantee safety", and checkpoints are not version control. That makes the guide's habits-first framing not just a stylistic choice but the only position the evidence supports: a student who attempts first, keeps instructions short, keeps git and backups out of an agent's reach, borrows patterns rather than assets, and sets model and effort before starting a session is protected regardless of which toggle a vendor renames next month.

The second implication is editorial. Roughly half the cited product facts carry relative dates, version numbers in the v2.1.2xx-v2.1.3xx range, or names that changed within the last year (routines to scheduled tasks, ChatGPT agent to ChatGPT Work, Gems to skills with removal on 17 December 2026). The research-backed habits (practice testing, attempt-first, least-privilege connectors, the lethal trifecta, image-then-text, borrow structure not expression) will outlast those product facts, so the guide should print the habits as the body and treat every tool detail as a dated, replaceable example, with the gaps above (Claude Styles, Custom GPTs, worktrees, UK GUI law, numeric plan caps) closed before anything is stated about them.
